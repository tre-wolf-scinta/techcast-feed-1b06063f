Roll-Up Summary Fields and the Lookup Trap

Maya wants one number on the Account page: the total budget of every open Project under that account, with no code. Project is a custom object with a lookup to Account. She starts a new roll-up summary field, looks for Project in the summarized object list, and it is not there. Why not?

Think of a restaurant tab. Every dish you order becomes a line on one table's bill, and the register recomputes the total at the bottom every time a line is added, changed, or removed. The bill is the parent, the dishes are the children, and the total is the roll-up. The register can only do this because each dish belongs to exactly one bill and cannot exist without it. On Salesforce, that strict ownership is a master-detail relationship, and a roll-up summary field is the total line that the platform maintains for you.

Project to Account is a lookup relationship, not master-detail, so the platform will not maintain that total. One exception to know for the exam: Opportunity to Account is also a lookup, but Salesforce makes a special case and lets Account roll up Opportunity values. Opportunity can also roll up its products, and Campaign can roll up its campaign members. Maya switches to the objects where it does. She has a custom Invoice object, and under it an Invoice Line object with a master-detail field pointing at Invoice. Each line has an Amount, a Quantity, a Ship Date, and a Status picklist. She creates a roll-up summary field on Invoice called Total Billed, picks Invoice Line as the summarized object, chooses SUM, and picks Amount as the field to total. Then she adds a filter so only lines where Status equals Billed count. From now on, whenever a line is inserted, updated, deleted, or undeleted, the platform recalculates Total Billed. The field is read-only on the parent and cannot be edited by hand.

She can summarize four ways. COUNT gives the number of matching child records. SUM adds a number, currency, or percent field. MIN and MAX work on numbers, currency, percents, and dates, so Latest Ship Date is a MAX over Ship Date. The matching rows can be filtered on child field values, which is why the Billed filter works.

Now the limits. Maya tries to sum a formula field on Invoice Line that pulls the discount rate from the parent Invoice. It is not in the list. A roll-up cannot summarize a formula field that references another object or a global merge field like the current user, because the platform cannot track when that value changes. Long text areas and multi-select picklists are out too. Next she wants Average Line Amount, and there is no AVG function. The exam answer is two roll-ups, a SUM of Amount and a COUNT of lines, plus a formula field on Invoice that divides one by the other. And for the original Account total of project budgets, the answer is Apex or a record-triggered flow, or converting the relationship to master-detail, which is only possible if every existing child already has a parent populated. One more figure worth holding: an object gets 25 roll-up summary fields by default.

So the rule: a roll-up needs master-detail, apart from those few standard exceptions, gives you count, sum, min, and max with an optional filter, and anything beyond that, whether an average, a lookup, or a cross-object formula, means a formula on top or automation of your own.
