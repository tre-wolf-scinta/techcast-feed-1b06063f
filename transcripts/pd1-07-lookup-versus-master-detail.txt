Lookup vs Master-Detail

You're adding a relationship field to a custom object and Salesforce asks you to pick: lookup or master-detail. Both store the parent record's ID on the child. So why does the exam care so much about which one you choose?

Maya is building a billing app with a custom Invoice object and a custom Invoice Line object, and every line belongs to exactly one invoice. Think of a master-detail child as a chapter inside a book. The chapter has no life of its own: whoever owns the book owns the chapter, and if you shred the book, the chapter goes with it. A lookup is a footnote that cites some other book. Throw that other book away and the footnote points at nothing, but the page it sits on is still there.

Maya makes Invoice Line a master-detail child of Invoice, and four things change at once. The Invoice field on the line becomes required, and the parent can't be changed later unless Maya turns on allow reparenting. The line loses its owner field. Ownership and sharing come from the invoice, so if you can see the invoice you can see its lines, and the line's org-wide default is simply controlled by parent. Deleting an invoice cascades to its lines, and undeleting it brings them back. And Maya can put a roll-up summary field on Invoice, a sum of line amounts or a count of lines, and the platform keeps it current. Roll-ups exist only on the master side of a master-detail. A lookup gives you none of that by default. The field is optional, the child keeps its own owner and its own sharing, and when the parent is deleted the platform clears the field, or you can configure the lookup to block the deletion instead.

Now Maya needs to connect Invoice to Account. Account is standard, and a standard object can be the master of a custom detail, so master-detail is allowed here. But picture the cascade. Someone deletes an account and every invoice under it vanishes. Billing records have to outlive their parent, so this is a lookup, and that survival test is what the exam is usually probing when a scenario seems to have two right answers. The limits come up too. A custom object can hold at most two master-detail fields, which is exactly what a junction object uses. A custom object can never be the master of a standard object, because standard objects can't be the detail side. And conversion has rules. Lookup to master-detail works only if every existing record has a value in the field. Master-detail to lookup works only after you remove any roll-up summary fields from the master.

So the rule of thumb: if the child can't exist without the parent and should share its owner, its access, and its fate, use master-detail. If the child must survive the parent, use a lookup.
