Formula Fields: Computed on Read, Never Stored

When an opportunity's amount changes, why does its discounted total update instantly, with no trigger, no flow, and no save? That's a formula field, and the exam wants to know exactly what it can and can't do.

Maya, a sales rep, opens an opportunity worth 10,000 dollars with a 15 percent discount. Her admin added a field called Net Amount, defined as Amount times one minus the discount. Maya sees 8,500 dollars. She clicks into it to round it off and nothing happens. Formula fields are read-only. The only way to change the output is to change the inputs.

Think of the arrival time on a navigation app. Nothing anywhere stores six twelve PM. The app recomputes it from your position and speed every time you glance at the screen. A formula field is that arrival time: calculated at display time, never written to the database, and always derived from other fields.

That one fact explains most of the behavior. Because nothing is stored, the value is recalculated whenever it's read, in a page, a report, a list view, or a SOQL query. And because nothing changes in the database when Net Amount changes, a formula value changing never fires a trigger or a flow on its own. Formulas can also reach upward. Net Amount could pull the industry from the opportunity's account, and the region from that account's parent, hopping through lookups or master-detail links up to 10 relationships away. This is called a cross-object formula. Inside it you'll use the usual functions: IF and CASE for branching, ISBLANK and ISPICKVAL for checks, TEXT to turn a picklist into a string, TODAY and NOW for dates, and HYPERLINK or IMAGE for display. The return type is fixed when you create the field: checkbox, currency, date, date-time, number, percent, text, or time.

Now Maya's manager wants the total Net Amount across every opportunity on the account. A formula looks up, never down. It cannot see child records, so it cannot sum them. That job belongs to a roll-up summary field, which only works over a master-detail relationship. But roll-ups have their own rule: they refuse to summarize a formula that reaches across objects or depends on the clock through TODAY or NOW. So the admin keeps Net Amount simple, using only the opportunity's own fields, and the roll-up can aggregate it. There's a second wall. Formula text is capped at 3,900 characters, and the compiled formula at 5,000 bytes. A nested CASE over a 20-value picklist burns that fast, and splitting it into helper formula fields doesn't escape it, because any formula you reference counts toward the compiled size of the formula using it.

So when is a formula the right answer instead of a trigger? When the value can be derived from the record and its parents, nobody needs to edit it, nobody needs field history on it, and nothing needs to react when it changes. Reach for a trigger or a flow when the value must be stored, must update other records, must respond to an event, or needs child data or logic the limits won't allow.

A formula field is a live calculation, not a stored value: it reads up the relationship chain, never down it, and the moment you need to store or act on the result, you've left formula territory.
